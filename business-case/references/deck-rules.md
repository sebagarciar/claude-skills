# Deck rules

How a case deck looks and moves. These rules come from a real interview case. The user repeated each of
them several times, so apply them from the first build. Colours and fonts are not here: each
case picks its own look, usually close to the company's site.

## Plan each slide before building it

Write down five things first. If one is missing, the slide is not ready.

1. The title, as a full sentence that states the message.
2. The two or three blocks under it. They do not overlap and leave nothing out.
3. The one thing to highlight.
4. The visual type (see below).
5. What each press shows (HTML only).

- **Answer first.** At deck level, part level and slide level: the answer, then two or three
  reasons, then the proof.
- **Match the visual to the message.**
  - Compare two things: side by side, same scale.
  - Change over time: bars or a line by period.
  - Steps or a plan: boxes left to right, or one timeline with milestones on it.
  - Parts of a whole: one stacked bar, not a pie.
  - Place (countries, rollout order): a real map.
  - A concept: two or three labelled blocks, no fake data.
- **5-second test.** Hide the title. If a smart reader cannot guess it from the visual, change
  the visual, not the caption.

## Titles

- Two lines at most. Use the full slide width.
- Readable alone: someone who reads only the title gets the point.
- The title and the body never repeat each other. A closing line that restates the title goes.
- In a board section, titles are action titles (the decision or the finding, with its number).

## Text on the slide

- One idea per line. Bullets over sentences glued with full stops. Text that reads "written by
  a robot" (short fragments joined by points, several ideas in one paragraph) gets rewritten.
- Full role titles on slides, never short forms (Regional Operations Manager, not ROM).
- Highlight one key phrase at most per slide. Two highlighted ideas use two different colours.
- A big number gets one lead line and at most one detail line. Leave space between the number
  and its label so it does not look blurry.
- Too much text: cut to titles plus one short line each. Too little: bring back the one
  example that makes the idea understandable. Show both versions when unsure.

## Layout

- **Same height.** Rows in a table have the same height (the header row can be smaller).
  Labels, headlines and bullets in sibling cards start at the same height.
- **No dancing.** Consecutive slides that show related numbers (before and after) use the same
  layout and the same order, so nothing jumps when moving between them.
- **One grid.** The title sits in the same place on every slide.
- If a slide is full, split it or move detail to the annex. Never shrink the text to fit.

## Footers, assumptions and the annex

- Footers hold only sources or assumptions. Never extra content or explanations.
- Long assumptions go to a backup slide. The footer has a link to it, and the backup slide has
  a link back to the slide it came from. Every annex slide has the back link.
- Every mention of the spreadsheet is a hyperlink to it.
- Every annex slide must be linked from somewhere. Delete orphans.

## Structure

- Cover: company name or logo top left, the case title, the user's name and the date at the
  bottom. No animation.
- Agenda: the parts as a plain list, with a time per part. No slogan.
- Each part opens with a divider that says what the part covers, not "Asked: ...".
- A board section opens with an executive summary: the ask, the result, the cost, and what
  each number rests on.
- Close the case with one simple closure slide before the personal close.

## HTML decks

- One file that opens with a double click. A fixed 16:9 stage scaled to the window, so no
  scrolling inside a slide.
- Keys: arrows to move, F for full screen, number keys 1 to 4 jump to each part's divider.
  No on-screen Grid or Full buttons.
- The URL hash follows the slide, so a link opens on that slide.
- **Presses.** One press per spoken idea, never a timer. Group what is said in one breath.
  Do not overdo it: too many presses is tiring. The final count is set in rehearsal, by speech.
- No presses on the cover, the agenda, the annex or a board (steerco) section. A board deck
  is read, not performed.
- Step through every press forward and back before calling it done. No dead press, no old
  state left behind, nothing running off the stage.
