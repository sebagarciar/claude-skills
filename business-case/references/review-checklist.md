# Review checklist

Run it once after the first full draft, and again only on slides that changed. Report each
check as pass or fail, with the slide and a proposed fix. Do not fix before the user picks.

## 1. The brief

- Every question in the brief checklist has a slide that answers it, in the brief's words and
  units.
- Nothing large on the slides answers a question the brief did not ask.
- Each part works on its own. No part leans on a number from a later part.

## 2. The reviewer

- The deck uses the reviewer's own words where the transcript has them.
- Nothing contradicts what the reviewer said (how the role works, the team, the pricing).
- Nothing promises more than a first-time person in the role could deliver.

## 3. The numbers

- Every number on a slide matches the spreadsheet: same value, rounding and unit.
- Every number can be traced on screen or in a linked backup.
- One return metric in the brief's units. List every multiple, % and ratio in the deck. If
  there are more than two, cut.
- The headline rests on what is measured, not on the weakest assumption. State what share of
  the headline is assumed.
- Each claim passes "can this ever be false?".

## 4. The timeline

- List every day, week and month in the deck in one table, in order. Check that no event
  happens before the data for it can exist, and that the same event never has two dates.

## 5. The partner read

Read the deck as a senior partner and as the final reader (for a board section, the CFO):

- Read the titles only. Do they tell the whole story?
- Which slide would the reader attack first, and does the deck answer it?
- Is any slide too packed for its audience?
- Is there jargon, a code word or a label that needs explaining?

## 6. The user can defend it

- For each slide, the user can say its point in one sentence.
- List the terms or numbers they asked about during the build. Each one is now either plain on
  the slide or cut.
