# Eliciting new stories from Seba

Phase 2 exists so the prep is not capped by what Seba has already written down. The quality of
the questions decides whether he gets a usable story or a shrug.

## Show the gap first

Before asking anything, show the scorecard: the competencies this role will probe, and where
the evidence is strong, thin or missing. He answers much better when he can see why the
question is being asked.

```
Competency                     Evidence
Pricing strategy               Strong — S1, S2, S3
Managing a direct team         Thin — U8 only, 2 people, no detail on how
Handling a failed launch       None
Influencing without authority  Strong — U5, U3
```

## How to ask

**Ask the interviewer's question, not a category.** Not "do you have anything on resilience".
Ask "tell me about a time a launch you owned did not work. What did you do in the first week
after you knew?" He answers a real question far better than a label.

**Ask one at a time.** Batching five questions gets five short answers.

**Anchor to what is already known.** "You supervised two people on the demand tool. What was the
hardest conversation you had with one of them?" beats a cold prompt, and it mines depth out of
material already in the bank.

**Go after the specific over the general.** If an answer is abstract, ask for the moment: what
was actually said, what did he decide, what happened next.

**Always ask for the number.** What changed, by how much, over what period. If he does not know,
record that it is unquantified rather than inventing a figure.

**Always ask what he would do differently.** That line is what makes a story usable for a
failure question later, and it is what senior interviewers listen for.

## Types worth mining, because they are usually missing

Achievements are always over-represented and everything else is thin. Push on:

- A failure he owned, and what it cost.
- Feedback that stung and was right.
- A conflict with a peer or a boss, and how it ended.
- A decision made without enough information.
- Something he stopped, killed, or said no to.
- A time he changed his own mind.
- Managing someone who was not performing.
- Work that succeeded because of someone else, which is what a good "tell me about your team"
  answer is built from.

## Writing it down

Write every answer straight into `job_search/story-bank.md` in the format in
`story-bank-format.md`. Use his words. Tighten grammar and cut filler, but do not restyle a
story into corporate prose: it has to sound like him when he says it out loud.

Per `MEMORY.md`, when the raw material is Seba's own words the first pass is grammar and
clarity only. Flag any structural change as a question rather than rewriting silently.

Show him what was written before moving on.
